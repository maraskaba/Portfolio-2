Environmental incident management system

Redesign of a legacy incident-reporting platform into a mobile-first, offline-capable experience for field operations.

Client YPF
Year 2022
Scope UX Design / UI Design / Agile (Scrum)

Context

YPF had an environmental incident management platform designed for desktop, built around long forms.

In practice, field operators used it from their phones, reporting incidents directly on-site, often with limited connectivity.

Challenge

Align the experience with real conditions of use. The existing tool was a long form with no clear structure and no offline support. For someone reporting an environmental incident in the field, under pressure and on a phone, this created concrete problems:

  • It was easy to lose progress or make mistakes.
  • Operators didn't understand where they were in the process.
  • There was no reliable way to handle poor or no connectivity.

Design approach

The project covered structure, flows, UI, and copy. I worked closely with the YPF team, under an agile approach that allowed us to iterate and progressively roll out improvements to the system:

  • I proposed turning the long form into a step-by-step flow, making the process clearer and easier to follow.
  • I designed two separate flows: one for connected scenarios and one for offline use.
  • I defined how the sync system worked together with the development team.
  • I worked with the YPF team on defining and refining the copy, especially for sync states and system messages.
  • I integrated into the team's agile workflow, adapting the design process to sprints: starting from a general layout and progressively refining each section while the team implemented in parallel.
  • I coordinated design work with the YPF team throughout the process, acting as the main point of contact from the consultancy.

The solution

The redesigned platform replaced a single, overwhelming form with a guided, step-by-step flow, built for mobile use in remote environments.

Navigation became clearer, progress was made visible, and the experience aligned with how incident reporting actually happens in the field.

Results

  • A general layout was delivered as a starting point, which the YPF team began implementing right away.
  • Work proceeded in sprints, progressively refining individual sections while development ran in parallel.
  • The modular structure let the team adopt changes incrementally without blocking implementation.
  • The client was satisfied with the process and the instructions provided for implementation.
