Movistar

Unified sign-up & login validation

We validated a unified login and sign-up flow to catch friction points before development.

Client Movistar
Year 2022
Scope Usability testing / Prototyping

Context

Movistar wanted to unify the access experience for different types of users, products, and services within a single system.

The challenge was to validate an architecture capable of integrating different account types, products, and authentication methods within one flow, and to reduce friction before implementation.

I ran remote user sessions to validate a flow proposed by Movistar's UX team.

The problem

The project's complexity wasn't just in the screens, but in how users could understand the system's logic and the relationships between accounts, lines, products, and authentication methods.

The flow had to account for:

  • Individual and corporate users.
  • Mobile lines and home products (WiFi, TV).
  • First-party and third-party products.
  • Authentication via SMS, or username and password.
  • Multiple identity validation methods (KYC).
  • Accounts tied to a national ID (individuals) and a tax ID (companies).

The main challenge was validating whether users understood:

  • How to access their services.
  • What they could manage within a single session.
  • How the associations between accounts, lines, and products worked.

Methodology

18 moderated remote sessions with individual and corporate users to validate the proposal received from Movistar.

I took part in moderating sessions and analyzing insights with the team to identify friction points, validate flows, and surface opportunities for improvement.

Functional prototype (HTML, PHP, JS)

For this project I built a high-fidelity functional prototype based on Movistar's team designs. Given the complexity of the flows and the limitations of traditional prototyping tools, I built it in HTML, PHP, and JavaScript, capable of simulating conditions close to production.

Building the prototype itself also helped surface inconsistencies and opportunities for improvement in the flow documentation, significantly raising the quality of the insights gathered during testing.

Unlike a traditional prototype, this environment made it possible to:

  • Simulate real interactions: working form fields, states, validations, and errors.
  • Integrate real SMS: users received codes on their own devices.
  • Evaluate more realistic performance: load and response times similar to production.

Key findings

  • Many of the issues found weren't related to visual UI, but to mismatches between users' mental model and the system's logic.
  • Users didn't always understand when they should log in with their own data versus the service holder's data.
  • Users interpreted labels literally. "Add more lines" was understood only as adding mobile lines.
  • Most users logged in via SMS and avoided traditional username-and-password login.
  • Users tended to assume the system was failing when the flow didn't respond as expected.
  • The concept of "national ID, tax ID, or both" was ambiguous within users' mental model.

Results

The validation process made it possible to:

  • Detect conceptual friction points before development.
  • Redefine labels and the service grouping logic.
  • Confirm SMS as the primary access method.
  • Identify specific limitations in corporate flows.
  • Improve feedback and operational clarity in critical scenarios.

Conclusions

  • Early validation made it possible to detect structural friction before implementation and to make product decisions backed by real user behavior.
  • Using a functional prototype was decisive: by replicating conditions close to production, users interacted with the system genuinely, significantly raising the quality of the findings obtained.
  • The study confirmed there was no need to rethink the overall architecture. The main improvements needed to focus on communication, feedback, and the visual clarity of the flows.
