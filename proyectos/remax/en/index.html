RE/MAX

Lead management system

Redesign of RE/MAX Argentina's lead management process. From an email-based flow across 160+ offices to a centralized, traceable system.

Client Remax
Year 2022
Scope Product design / UX Research

Context

RE/MAX Argentina received property inquiries through a web form that fed into a manual workflow across three groups: the central team, RE/MAX offices, and agents. Each lead moved through email, with no system providing visibility or continuity to the process.

The challenge

The challenge was to propose a flow within the existing system (Red RE/MAX) that could be adopted in stages, without forcing an overnight cultural change.

This required understanding how the three groups actually worked, identifying the real friction points, and designing a solution that supported the existing process while making it possible to organize and scale it.

My role

As part of the Kambrica team, my role combined research and product design. I conducted exploratory interviews with all three user profiles, led usability testing on the functional prototype, and translated the findings into the system's design decisions.

Goals

Business goals

  • Centralize lead management in a single platform (Red RE/MAX).
  • Provide traceability for the full cycle: Reception → Assignment → Acceptance → Contact → Conversion.
  • Lay the groundwork for future automatic assignment and for generating metrics to evaluate performance by office and by agent.

Experience goals

  • Reduce the manual coordination time between the central team and RE/MAX offices.
  • Give agents clarity on the expected actions at each step.
  • Establish a simple, scalable system that could accommodate new features without overwhelming agents.

Research and findings

The process began with in-depth interviews across the three groups: agents, RE/MAX office managers, and the central team. The goal was to map the existing flow end to end and identify the real friction points.

Methodology

  • I conducted exploratory interviews with teams from all three groups.
  • I mapped the complete flow, from inquiry to assignment.
  • I analyzed the parallel tools agents used outside the system.

Key findings

  • Agents only started using Red RE/MAX once a property had been secured. Everything before that lived in email and personal tools.
  • Office managers were explicitly resistant to automatic assignment: not all agents take on all properties, and "on-call" rotations have too many exceptions to automate easily.
  • Two of the five agents interviewed openly said they'd keep using their personal spreadsheets "just in case," even after the new system was in place.

Roadmap

Based on the findings, it was clear the solution couldn't be a fully automated system from day one. Office managers wouldn't accept it, agents wouldn't trust it, and the central team would risk a flawed rollout.

The decision was to design a system in four progressive stages, each one paving the way for the next. The scope of this stage was manual, but fully traceable, assignment.

Solution

I worked on designing a workflow integrated into the existing platform to centralize lead management, organized around three touchpoints:

  • The central team assigning leads to RE/MAX offices.
  • Office managers assigning leads to agents.
  • Agents accepting and managing the lead.

Key components

  • A lead list with filters and prioritization. Leads show a visible countdown timer.
  • An assignment system for offices and agents.
  • Information to decide who to assign: distance to the lead, number of previous sends and rejections, and eventually a score (for future automation).
  • Clear process states (received, assigned, contacted, converted, rejected).
  • An activity log for full traceability.

This turned scattered manual coordination into a centralized flow, with full traceability and ready for future automation.

Validation

I ran usability testing on the prototype with agents and office managers, covering different scenarios.

Usability results

  • All users operated the system without significant usability issues.
  • Points of confusion were resolved quickly and intuitively.
  • The process of rejecting/discarding a received lead was understood without friction.
  • The countdown timer was correctly interpreted as a signal of urgency and reassignment.

Identified opportunities for change

  • Client data was hidden until formal acceptance of the lead. Agents suggested calling before accepting.
  • Copy and label adjustments to bring clarity to each action.
  • New states (like "following up") were added, which agents explicitly requested.

Results

The project delivered a designed and validated system:

  • The system organizes leads through clear process states (received, assigned, contacted, converted, rejected).
  • It enables assignment to offices and agents.
  • It prioritizes urgent inquiries.
  • It provides full traceability through the activity log.
